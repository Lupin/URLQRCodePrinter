URLQRCodePrinter

Collect addresses from your browser. They stay on your machine, and you print them as QR codes. The extension reads the address and title of the page or link you target, and saves them. The app lays out what you print: sticky labels, a Niimbot printer over Bluetooth, or an image export. There is no account to create, the publisher runs no server, and nothing is measured.

Install the extension Try it in your browser GitHub repo

What it does

  • Collect in one gesture

    Right-click a page or a link, or use the toolbar button. The title is kept, so the label stays recognisable.

  • Compose the sheet

    Labels are laid out for the medium you are targeting, then printed to PDF. The preview measures the real rendering, it is not an estimate.

  • Print directly

    To a Niimbot D110 or M2 printer over Bluetooth, with no intermediate file.

  • Shorten for a smaller QR

    A shorter link gives a smaller code that scans faster and fits on a narrow label.

  • Export

    CSV, Markdown, JSON, or the PNG images as an archive, so you can take your data away at any time in a readable format.

  • Nothing to run server-side

    The web app works on its own. The extension adds collection from the browser, into the same collection.

Why I built it

I built this extension for my own notebook. I keep my research and my finds on paper, and I used to copy the addresses into it by hand. Writing them took time, and finding them again took just as long. I started by printing the QR codes one at a time on a small Niimbot, then decided it could all be done automatically.

It matters most for what you want to keep within reach: a research paper, a product page, a video that stayed with you. You scan the label, and when you come back to the notebook, whatever caught your attention is there to find and to share.

- Gaël A.G. -

Privacy

What is read. The address and title of the page you act on, only that one and only at the moment you ask.

What is not done. No access to your history, no reading in the background, no account, no server run by the publisher, no analytics, no advertising, no selling of data. No code is loaded remotely: everything ships with the app.

The only network request. If you ask for a link to be shortened, that address is sent to the service you chose: TinyURL, is.gd, v.gd or spoo.me. Without that action, no request is made.

What it does not do

Better said here than discovered in use:

  • Safari and Firefox cannot print directly. Neither implements Web Bluetooth, and Safari imposes its engine on every browser on iPhone and iPad. Image export remains available everywhere.
  • Brave needs a manual step. Web Bluetooth is disabled by default there, behind a flag to enable in brave://flags.
  • The web app and the extension do not share data. The first stores in the browser, the second in the extension's storage. Use one or the other; export is the bridge.
  • The extension is published on the Chrome Web Store. It targets Chrome, Brave and Edge. The Safari version is compiled from source, and the web app stays usable without installing anything.
